🌳 The Sauce Family Tree

Auguste Escoffier codified five mother sauces (sauces mères) in the early 20th century. Every classical French sauce derives from one of these five bases. Understanding the mother sauces means you can reverse-engineer any classical recipe.

Béchamel
Milk + white roux → Mornay, Cream Sauce, Soubise, Nantua
Velouté
Light stock + blonde roux → Suprême, Allemande, Vin Blanc, Normande
Espagnole
Brown stock + brown roux → Demi-glace, Bordelaise, Chasseur, Robert, Madeira
Sauce Tomat
Tomatoes + stock → Tomato Coulis, Arrabbiata, Provençale, Portuguese
Hollandaise
Egg yolks + clarified butter → Béarnaise, Maltaise, Mousseline, Choron

🥛 1. Béchamel — The White Sauce

Béchamel
Mother Sauce · Milk + White Roux
The simplest mother sauce. A white roux (equal parts butter and flour) cooked with milk, seasoned with nutmeg. Forms the base for most creamy white sauces.
Ingredients (500 ml)
  • 500 ml whole milk
  • 30 g unsalted butter
  • 30 g plain flour
  • Salt, white pepper, freshly grated nutmeg
Method
  1. Melt butter in a heavy saucepan over medium heat.
  2. Add flour and stir constantly for 1–2 minutes until pale (white roux).
  3. Remove from heat. Gradually whisk in warm milk, a little at a time.
  4. Return to medium heat, stirring constantly until thick and smooth (~5 min).
  5. Season with salt, white pepper, and a pinch of nutmeg.
Classic derivatives
Mornay (+ Gruyère) Cream Sauce (+ cream) Soubise (+ sweated onion) Nantua (+ crayfish butter)
Sauce Mornay
Béchamel Derivative · Cheese Sauce
The most famous Béchamel derivative. Essential for gratin dishes, croque monsieur, and cauliflower cheese.
Method (from Béchamel)
  1. Make 500 ml Béchamel.
  2. Remove from heat, add 2 egg yolks whisked with 50 ml cream.
  3. Stir in 50 g grated Gruyère and 25 g Parmesan.
  4. Season and use immediately, or finish under the grill for a gratin.

🍗 2. Velouté — The Velvet Sauce

Velouté
Mother Sauce · Light Stock + Blonde Roux
Made with light (white) stock — chicken, veal, or fish — thickened with a blonde roux. Richer than Béchamel, lighter than Espagnole. The base for the most elegant French sauces.
Ingredients (500 ml)
  • 500 ml white chicken or veal stock (hot)
  • 30 g unsalted butter
  • 30 g plain flour
  • Salt, white pepper
Method
  1. Make a blonde roux: cook butter and flour 2–3 min until lightly golden.
  2. Gradually whisk in hot stock.
  3. Simmer 20 minutes, skimming occasionally, until velvety and coating.
  4. Strain through a fine sieve.
Classic derivatives
Suprême (chicken velouté + cream) Allemande (+ egg yolk liaison) Vin Blanc (fish velouté + white wine) Normande (fish + mushrooms + cream)
Sauce Suprême
Velouté Derivative · Classic Fine Dining
The most luxurious white sauce — reduced chicken velouté finished with cream. Served with poached chicken, quenelles, and vegetables.
Method
  1. Reduce 500 ml chicken velouté by one third.
  2. Add 150 ml double cream and reduce to a coating consistency.
  3. Finish with 30 g cold butter, swirled in off heat.
  4. Season and strain.

🥩 3. Espagnole — The Brown Sauce

Espagnole
Mother Sauce · Brown Stock + Brown Roux
The most complex mother sauce. Brown roux with brown veal stock, mirepoix, tomato purée, and a bouquet garni. Reduced to a rich, deeply savoury base. Always the starting point for demi-glace.
Ingredients (1 litre)
  • 1.5 litres brown veal stock
  • 60 g butter + 60 g flour (brown roux)
  • 100 g each: diced onion, carrot, celery (mirepoix)
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • Bouquet garni (thyme, bay, parsley stalks)
Method
  1. Make brown roux: cook butter and flour, stirring, until deep brown (8–10 min).
  2. Add mirepoix and cook until softened and browned.
  3. Add tomato purée, cook 2 minutes.
  4. Gradually whisk in hot stock. Add bouquet garni.
  5. Simmer 1–2 hours, skimming regularly, until reduced by half.
  6. Strain. This is Espagnole — reduce further for Demi-glace.
Classic derivatives
Demi-glace (reduced 50%) Bordelaise (+ red wine + shallots) Chasseur (+ mushrooms + white wine) Robert (+ onion + mustard + vinegar) Madeira (+ Madeira wine)
Demi-glace
Espagnole Derivative · The Ultimate Base
Equal parts Espagnole and brown stock, reduced by half to a glossy, intensely flavoured sauce. The foundation of all classic brown sauces. Any restaurant serving proper French cuisine makes this weekly.
Method
  1. Combine 500 ml Espagnole + 500 ml brown veal stock.
  2. Simmer over medium-low heat, skimming constantly.
  3. Reduce by half until glossy and coats a spoon thickly (~45 min).
  4. Strain through fine muslin. Season.
  5. Use as a base or reduce further for a glaze.

🍅 4. Sauce Tomat — The Tomato Sauce

Sauce Tomat
Mother Sauce · Tomatoes + Stock
The French classical version is more complex than Italian tomato sauce — includes a roux and stock. Escoffier's version used salt pork, vegetables, and long simmering. Modern versions are simpler.
Ingredients (modern, 500 ml)
  • 1 kg ripe tomatoes (or 800 g tinned whole)
  • 1 onion, 2 garlic cloves, 1 carrot — diced
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 150 ml chicken or veal stock
  • Thyme, bay leaf, pinch of sugar
Method
  1. Sweat onion, carrot, garlic in oil until soft (8 min).
  2. Add tomatoes, stock, herbs, and sugar.
  3. Simmer uncovered 25–30 minutes until thick.
  4. Pass through a mouli or blender, then strain for a fine sauce.
  5. Adjust seasoning.
Derivatives
Provençale (+ olives, capers, herbs) Portuguese (+ sautéed onion, garlic) Arrabbiata (+ chilli, garlic) Coulis (strained, raw or cooked)

🥚 5. Hollandaise — The Emulsified Butter Sauce

Hollandaise
Mother Sauce · Egg Yolks + Clarified Butter
A warm emulsified sauce. Egg yolks are whisked with a reduction of white wine and vinegar, then clarified butter is incorporated slowly to form a stable emulsion. The most technically demanding mother sauce — temperature control is critical.
Ingredients (4 portions)
  • 3 egg yolks
  • 250 g clarified (or whole) unsalted butter, warm
  • Reduction: 2 tbsp white wine vinegar + 2 tbsp white wine + 1 shallot + 6 peppercorns → reduce to 1 tbsp
  • Lemon juice, salt, cayenne pepper
Method
  1. Strain the reduction into a bowl. Add egg yolks.
  2. Whisk over barely simmering water (bain-marie) until thick, pale, and tripled (ribbon stage). Do NOT let the bowl touch the water.
  3. Remove from heat. Whisk in clarified butter drop by drop at first, then in a thin stream.
  4. Season with lemon, salt, and cayenne. Serve immediately or keep warm (max 65°C).
Classic derivatives
Béarnaise (+ tarragon reduction) Maltaise (+ blood orange juice) Mousseline (+ whipped cream) Choron (Béarnaise + tomato) Foyot (Béarnaise + demi-glace)
Béarnaise
Hollandaise Derivative · The Steak Sauce
The most celebrated derivative of Hollandaise. The tarragon-forward reduction gives it a distinctive herbaceous, anise-like flavour. Essential with entrecôte, châteaubriand, and grilled fish.
Method (differs from Hollandaise)
  1. Make a new reduction: 50 ml white wine + 50 ml tarragon vinegar + 2 shallots + fresh tarragon + peppercorns → reduce to 2 tbsp.
  2. Proceed exactly as Hollandaise from this reduction.
  3. Finish with chopped fresh tarragon and chervil.
  4. Do NOT add lemon juice (it would clash with the tarragon).

⚡ Modern & Butter Sauces

Beurre Blanc
Butter Emulsion · Loire Valley Origin
A classically French butter sauce from the Loire. Not technically a "mother sauce" but arguably the most versatile modern sauce. Brilliant with fish, vegetables, and seafood.
Ingredients
  • 200 g cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 3 shallots, finely chopped
  • 100 ml dry white wine
  • 50 ml white wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp double cream (stabiliser, optional)
Method
  1. Simmer shallots, wine, and vinegar until reduced to 2 tbsp (almost dry).
  2. Add cream and reduce briefly.
  3. Over very low heat, whisk in cold butter cube by cube.
  4. The sauce should be thick, creamy, and pale. Strain if desired.
  5. Season with salt and white pepper. Serve immediately.
Pan Sauce / Jus Lié
Modern Technique · From Fond
The simplest and most powerful modern sauce technique. Deglaze the fond (the caramelised bits left in a pan after searing meat) with wine or stock and reduce. The Maillard reaction creates complex flavour in minutes.
Method
  1. Remove the cooked protein and rest it. Pour off excess fat, leave the fond.
  2. Deglaze with 100 ml wine or stock, scraping the fond vigorously.
  3. Add 200 ml demi-glace or strong stock.
  4. Reduce by half until coating consistency.
  5. Mount with 30 g cold butter off the heat. Season.

🔬 The Science Behind Sauce-Making

Emulsification: Hollandaise, Béarnaise, and Beurre Blanc are emulsions — fat dispersed in water using lecithin in egg yolks as an emulsifier. Temperature must stay below 70°C or proteins denature and the sauce "breaks" (splits into greasy puddles).
Roux ratios: Thin sauce: 20 g butter + 20 g flour per 500 ml. Medium: 30 g each. Thick: 40 g each. Always cook the roux first to eliminate the raw flour taste — minimum 2 minutes for white, 10 minutes for brown.
Reduction = concentration: Reducing a sauce by half doubles the intensity of flavour. This is why demi-glace (reduced Espagnole) has such extraordinary depth. Time and patience is the secret ingredient.
