🎯 AI Strategy — The Business Framework

Successful AI adoption requires a deliberate strategy. Ad-hoc AI projects without strategic alignment create technical debt, governance gaps, and low ROI. A structured approach follows four steps:

1. Define the AI Vision
What business outcomes does AI serve? Articulate a clear, executive-endorsed AI vision tied to corporate strategy. "We will use AI to reduce customer churn by 20% in 18 months" — not "we will implement AI".
2. Identify Use Cases
Systematic scanning for AI opportunities. Prioritise using a feasibility-impact matrix. Quick wins for momentum; strategic bets for transformation.
3. Build the Foundation
Data quality, governance, cloud infrastructure, skills, culture. AI is only as good as its data. Without data foundations, AI projects fail.
4. Scale and Govern
Pilot → validate → scale. Establish AI governance, model monitoring, ethical review boards. The hardest part is not building AI — it's running it responsibly at scale.

💡 Where AI Creates Business Value

⚡ Automate Repetitive Tasks
Invoice processing, data entry, document classification, customer routing. Frees humans for higher-value work. Immediate ROI measurable in FTE savings.
🧠 Augment Human Decisions
Credit scoring, medical diagnosis support, demand forecasting. Human remains accountable; AI improves accuracy and speed. "Human-in-the-loop" design.
🚀 Create New Products
AI-native services that weren't possible before: personalised recommendations, real-time translation, predictive maintenance. Competitive differentiation.
😊 Improve Customer Experience
Personalisation, 24/7 chatbot support, sentiment-driven service routing, proactive outreach. NPS improvement often the KPI.
🔍 Unlock Insights
Pattern detection in data too large or complex for humans. Fraud detection, supply chain optimisation, equipment failure prediction.
⚙️ Optimise Operations
Resource scheduling, energy optimisation, inventory management, process bottleneck detection. Operational efficiency gains.

📊 AI Use Case Prioritisation Matrix

✅ High Impact + High Feasibility → Do Now
Data is available, skills exist, clear ROI. E.g. invoice automation, customer churn prediction with existing CRM data.
🔶 High Impact + Low Feasibility → Invest & Plan
Strategically important but needs data infrastructure or skills building. E.g. predictive maintenance requiring new IoT sensors.
📋 Low Impact + High Feasibility → Consider
Easy to do but limited ROI. Only pursue if it builds AI maturity or capabilities for bigger projects later.
❌ Low Impact + Low Feasibility → Avoid
Neither strategically important nor technically achievable. Decline or defer.

Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) for AI: Include ALL costs — data labelling, model training compute, inference costs, monitoring, retraining, human review, model governance, and decommissioning. AI projects routinely underestimate the 70% of cost that is post-deployment.

🏛️ AI Governance

AI Ethics Committee
Cross-functional: legal, compliance, data science, business, HR. Reviews high-risk AI use cases before deployment. Sets policy and adjudicates edge cases.
Model Governance Lifecycle
Development → Review → Approval → Production → Monitoring → Retraining → Retirement. Each stage needs documented criteria. No model escapes governance.
Bias & Fairness Review
Test model performance across demographic groups before deployment. Disparate impact analysis. Ongoing monitoring after go-live for model drift.
Explainability Requirements
High-stakes decisions (credit, hiring, medical) require explainable outputs. "The AI said no" is never acceptable to regulators. Document model limitations.

⚖️ EU AI Act — Risk Categories

The EU AI Act (in force 2024) classifies AI systems by risk level. Higher risk = stricter requirements. Understanding this is critical for any business deploying AI in Europe.

🚫 Unacceptable Risk
BANNED. Social scoring by governments, real-time remote biometric surveillance in public spaces (with exceptions), manipulation of subconscious behaviour, exploitation of vulnerabilities.
⚠️ High Risk
STRICT REQUIREMENTS. Biometric identification, critical infrastructure, education, employment decisions, access to essential services, law enforcement, justice. Must have human oversight, risk management, documentation.
📋 Limited Risk
TRANSPARENCY OBLIGATIONS. Chatbots must identify as AI. Deepfakes must be labelled. Emotion recognition systems must disclose usage.
✅ Minimal Risk
NO SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS. Spam filters, AI in games, recommendation systems. No mandatory obligations beyond general law.
