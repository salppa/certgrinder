EU AI Act

EU Artificial Intelligence Act — Core Framework

What is the EU AI Act?

The world's first comprehensive legal framework for AI, adopted by the European Parliament in March 2024 and entering into force August 2024. It applies to providers, deployers, importers and distributors of AI systems in the EU — regardless of where the provider is established. Finnish organisations deploying Azure AI services are subject to the Act as deployers.

Risk-Based Classification System

Obligations for High-Risk AI Deployers (Finnish Organisations)

  • Implement a risk management system before deployment
  • Ensure human oversight measures are in place
  • Log system activities — ensure traceability
  • Register system in the EU AI Act database (when applicable)
  • Conduct fundamental rights impact assessments (FRIA) for public bodies
  • Designate an EU representative if the provider is outside the EU
  • Inform and train staff who operate or oversee high-risk AI

GPAI Model Rules — Why Azure OpenAI Is in Scope

General Purpose AI (GPAI) models like GPT-4 must provide: technical documentation, training data summaries, copyright compliance policies, and safety evaluations. Models trained with >10²⁵ FLOPs face additional systemic risk obligations. As an Azure AI deployer building on GPAI models, your organisation must ensure the upstream provider (Microsoft/OpenAI) is compliant and obtain evidence of that compliance.

GDPR × AI

GDPR Obligations When Deploying AI

How GDPR Interacts with AI Systems

GDPR applies to any AI system processing personal data. The EU AI Act and GDPR are cumulative — both apply simultaneously. In Finland, the Data Protection Ombudsman (Tietosuojavaltuutettu) enforces GDPR and cooperates with AI oversight authorities.

Key GDPR Principles for AI Deployment

Art. 5
Data minimisation — AI must process only necessary personal data. Avoid training on excessive personal data.
Art. 22
Automated decision-making — individuals have the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects. Human review must be available.
Art. 25
Data protection by design and by default — privacy features must be built into AI systems from the start, not added later.
Art. 35
Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) — mandatory before deploying AI systems that involve systematic profiling, biometrics, or large-scale processing.
Art. 13/14
Transparency — individuals must be informed that AI is being used and how their data is processed. Plain-language explanation required.

Finnish-Specific Considerations

  • Finnish Data Protection Act (1050/2018) supplements GDPR with national specifications
  • Public sector AI must go through Tietosuojavaltuutettu consultation for new high-risk processing
  • Special categories of data (health, trade union membership, ethnicity) require explicit legal basis — common in HR AI tools
  • DigiFinland and Finnish government cloud strategy require data residency within EU/EEA — Azure Finnish data centres (Finland South/North) satisfy this
Azure AI

Azure AI Governance & Responsible AI Controls

Microsoft Responsible AI Standard

  • Fairness — AI must treat all people fairly, tested across demographic groups
  • Reliability & Safety — systems must behave as intended and be fail-safe
  • Privacy & Security — personal data protected by design
  • Inclusiveness — designed for all users regardless of ability
  • Transparency — explainability for AI-driven decisions
  • Accountability — humans must be accountable for AI systems

Azure AI Compliance Controls — EA Reference

Azure Policy
Enforce governance rules across all Azure AI resource deployments. Prevent non-compliant model deployments via policy deny effects.
Content Safety
Azure AI Content Safety service filters harmful outputs. Mandatory for customer-facing applications under responsible AI policy.
Managed Identity
Avoid credential exposure by using Managed Identity for Azure OpenAI access. Eliminates stored API key risk.
Private Endpoints
Azure OpenAI should be accessed via Private Endpoint in production, eliminating public internet exposure of sensitive AI inference traffic.
Customer Managed Keys
For sensitive data processed by Azure AI, use CMK (Customer Managed Keys) in Azure Key Vault so Microsoft cannot access encryption keys.
Audit Logging
Enable Azure Monitor and Diagnostic Settings for all Azure AI services. Log AI interactions for accountability and forensics per EU AI Act traceability requirements.

Azure OpenAI Data Residency

  • Prompts and completions are NOT stored by default in Azure OpenAI
  • When abuse monitoring is enabled, data may be retained up to 30 days by Microsoft
  • You can request to opt out of human review for abuse monitoring with Microsoft approval
  • Choose the Azure region closest to Finland (Sweden Central, North Europe) for data residency
  • Fine-tuned model data stays in your chosen region

Copilot for Microsoft 365 — Enterprise Controls

  • Copilot respects existing Microsoft 365 permissions — it won't surface data users can't normally access
  • Copilot interaction logs are stored in the user's Exchange Online mailbox (Copilot chat history)
  • Microsoft Purview can be used to apply retention labels and DLP policies to Copilot outputs
  • No customer data is used to train the underlying models (contractual guarantee in enterprise agreements)
  • Sensitivity labels from Microsoft Purview propagate into Copilot-generated content

Enterprise Architect AI Decision Frameworks

AI System Classification Checklist (for EA Gate Reviews)

  • Does the system make or support decisions affecting individuals' rights or access to services?
  • Does it process biometric, health, or other special category data?
  • Is it deployed in critical infrastructure, law enforcement, or education?
  • Does it operate autonomously without continuous human oversight?
  • Will it interact with vulnerable groups (children, elderly, minorities)?

→ If ANY answer is yes: initiate high-risk assessment track. Involve legal counsel and Data Protection Officer.

🇫🇮 Finland

Finnish National AI Context

Key Finnish Regulatory Bodies for AI

  • Tietosuojavaltuutettu (Data Protection Ombudsman) — GDPR enforcement, lead authority for many cross-border AI cases
  • Liikenne- ja viestintävirasto (Traficom) — designated as market surveillance authority under EU AI Act for several sectors
  • Kilpailu- ja kuluttajavirasto (KKV) — consumer protection aspects of AI
  • Finanssivalvonta (FIN-FSA) — AI in financial services (credit scoring, trading, insurance)

DigiFinland & Public Sector AI

  • Finnish government cloud strategy mandates EU data residency for all public sector data
  • DigiFinland services must comply with both EU AI Act and GDPR simultaneously
  • Public bodies deploying high-risk AI must conduct Fundamental Rights Impact Assessments (FRIA)
  • JulkICT (Government ICT) guidelines specify AI procurement requirements for state organisations
  • National AI strategy (Tekoälyohjelma) promotes responsible AI adoption with ethical guidelines

SAP + Azure AI in Finnish Enterprise Context

  • SAP S/4HANA on Azure: AI-assisted allocations (Universal Allocation) may qualify as high-risk if used for workforce cost decisions
  • SAP Joule (embedded AI) on Azure infrastructure is subject to both EU AI Act and SAP's responsible AI framework
  • Azure OpenAI integrated with SAP via API requires DPIA if processing employee personal data
  • Finnish Works Council Act (Yhteistoimintalaki) requires consultation before deploying AI-based HR tools
