🔑 What is SABSA?

SABSA (Sherwood Applied Business Security Architecture) is a business-driven security architecture framework. Unlike technology-first approaches, SABSA starts with the business need and traces everything — every control, every policy, every product — back to a measurable business attribute.

Created by John Sherwood. Holistic — covers People, Process, and Technology. Maps to ISO 27001, NIST, COBIT, and other standards. Used by large enterprises, governments, and regulated industries worldwide.

Core principle: Security is a business enabler, not a barrier. Every control exists to enable a business attribute. If you can't trace a control to a business requirement, you shouldn't have it.

📐 The SABSA Matrix

The SABSA Matrix is a 6×6 grid (6 layers × 6 columns = 36 cells). Each cell defines a specific architectural artefact. The rows move from abstract business context down to concrete operational detail.

The 6 Columns (What, How, Where, Who, When, Why)

📦 What — Assets
The inventory. What things need to be protected? At contextual level: the Business Domain. At component level: specific data packets and protocols.
⚙️ How — Process
The processes. How is work done? At contextual level: Business Activities. At component level: Security tools and mechanisms.
🗺️ Where — Geography
The locations. Where do things happen? At contextual level: Business Locations. At component level: Network nodes and interface specs.
👤 Who — Entity
The actors. Who is involved? At contextual level: People and Organisations. At component level: Certificates and specific credentials.
🕐 When — Time
The timing. When do things happen? At contextual level: Business Calendar / Timescales. At component level: Specific timestamps, session durations.
❓ Why — Motivation
The drivers. Why is this needed? At contextual level: Business Drivers (regulations, competition). At component level: Operational targets (e.g. 99.9% uptime SLA).

📊 Business Attributes & Risk

Business Attributes are the desired qualities of the business or system — things like "Private", "Traceable", "Available", "Authentic". They are identified by stakeholders and drive the entire security architecture.

🔍 Attributes Profiling
The process of translating business needs into measurable security attributes. Each attribute gets a proxy metric — something you can actually measure (e.g. "time to detect a breach" as proxy for "Traceable").
⚖️ Risk as Balance
SABSA views risk as both threat (negative) and opportunity (positive). Security architecture must mitigate threats and enable opportunities — not just say no.
🎯 Controls = Enablers
In SABSA, security controls are not restrictions — they are enablers. A control enables business attributes. If you can't trace a control to an attribute, it shouldn't exist.
📏 Control Objective
The goal a control is designed to achieve. Sits at the Conceptual layer in the "Why" column. Drives the detailed design of physical and component controls below it.

🔄 The SABSA Lifecycle

Architecture is never static. SABSA uses a four-phase lifecycle that repeats continuously — not a one-time project.

1
Strategy & Planning
Define business context, drivers, risk appetite. Contextual + Conceptual layers.
2
Design
Logical and Physical architecture. Trust models, mechanisms, domain structure.
3
Implement
Build or acquire. Component layer — specific products, configurations, deployments.
4
Manage & Measure
Operational layer. Prove attributes are met. Continuous monitoring. Feed back to Strategy.
Through-life Management: Architecture must be managed throughout its entire lifecycle, not just at initial deployment. Business drivers change → attributes change → architecture must adapt.

🔗 Traceability

Traceability is one of SABSA's most distinctive features. It ensures that every control exists for a reason (no gold-plating) and every requirement is addressed (no gaps).

↕️ Vertical Traceability
Links across layers — from business driver (top) down to the specific security tool (bottom). Ensures a firewall rule traces back to a business requirement.
↔️ Horizontal Traceability
Links within a layer — ensures the What, How, Where, Who, When, Why of a single layer are all consistent with each other. No internal contradictions.
⛔ Gold-plating
Adding controls that have no corresponding business requirement. Traceability prevents this — every control must trace upward to a driver. Wastes resources.
⚠️ Gaps
Business requirements that have no corresponding control. Vertical traceability catches these — every driver at the top must trace down to something concrete.

🏛️ Governance

🔑 SABSA Defined
SABSA = Sherwood Applied Business Security Architecture. Created by John Sherwood. Published in 1995, continuously evolved. Managed by the SABSA Institute.
📋 Stakeholder Role
Stakeholders (business owners) define the Business Attributes and Risk Appetite at the Contextual layer. The architect translates these requirements — not the other way around.
🤝 Framework Integration
SABSA is holistic and integrates with ISO 27001, NIST CSF, COBIT, TOGAF, and ITIL. It provides the "why and what" — other frameworks provide the "how to do it" detail.
🏢 Domain
A set of entities under a common trust and policy. Domains at the Logical layer define trust zones. Policies are consistent within a domain and explicitly managed at boundaries.
