⚖️ Governance vs Management — The Core Distinction

🏛️ Governance (EDM domain)
Set by the governing body (board, executives). Three activities:

Evaluate — assess options and trade-offs
Direct — set direction through priorities and policies
Monitor — verify performance against targets and compliance

5 objectives: EDM01–EDM05
⚙️ Management (APO/BAI/DSS/MEA domains)
Performed by management (C-suite, IT management). Four domains follow the Plan-Build-Run-Monitor model:

APO — Align, Plan, Organise (Plan)
BAI — Build, Acquire, Implement (Build)
DSS — Deliver, Service, Support (Run)
MEA — Monitor, Evaluate, Assess (Monitor)

35 objectives total
Key exam distinction: Governance = Evaluate, Direct, Monitor (EDM). Management = Plan, Build, Run, Monitor (APO, BAI, DSS, MEA). The board does governance. Management does management. They are separate but interlocked.

🎛️ The 11 Design Factors

COBIT 2019 is not one-size-fits-all. Design factors shape a tailored governance system for each enterprise.

#Design FactorWhat it determines
1Enterprise StrategyGrowth/innovation vs stability vs cost leadership vs client service
2Enterprise GoalsThe 13 COBIT enterprise goals most relevant to the organisation
3Risk ProfileTypes of IT-related risk the enterprise faces today
4I&T-Related IssuesCurrent pain points — security incidents, compliance failures, project overruns
5Threat LandscapeNormal vs high threat environment (e.g. financial sector = high threat)
6Compliance RequirementsRegulatory, legal, and contractual compliance obligations
7Role of ITSupport vs factory vs turnaround vs strategic role of IT
8Sourcing ModelIn-house vs outsourced vs cloud vs hybrid
9IT Implementation MethodsAgile vs waterfall vs DevOps vs hybrid
10Technology Adoption StrategyFirst mover vs follower vs slow adopter
11Enterprise SizeSmall vs medium vs large — drives capability level targets

🎯 Focus Areas

Digital Transformation
How governance adapts when IT becomes the business. New risks, new value streams, new operating models.
Cloud Computing
Governance of cloud services — vendor risk, data sovereignty, shared responsibility, cloud cost governance.
Cybersecurity
APO13 (Managed Security) and DSS05 (Managed Security Services) are the primary objectives. Risk-based approach.
Privacy
APO14 (Managed Data) covers privacy. Aligned with GDPR and similar regulations. Data classification and handling.
DevOps
How COBIT governance applies to fast-moving DevOps pipelines. BAI06 (IT Changes) and BAI07 (Change Acceptance) are key.
Small & Medium Enterprises
COBIT guidance for resource-constrained organisations. Prioritise objectives based on enterprise size design factor.

📊 The Goals Cascade

COBIT links stakeholder needs all the way down to specific governance objectives through a four-level cascade:

LevelDescriptionCount
Stakeholder NeedsWhat stakeholders (investors, customers, regulators) actually want — value, risk reduction, resource optimisationVaries
Enterprise GoalsHigh-level business goals that reflect stakeholder needs (e.g. "managed business risk", "customer-oriented service culture")13 goals
Alignment GoalsI&T-related goals that support enterprise goals (e.g. "security of information and processing infrastructure")13 goals
Governance & Management ObjectivesSpecific COBIT objectives (EDM, APO, BAI, DSS, MEA) that support alignment goals40 objectives
Exam tip: The Goals Cascade is how COBIT justifies governance investment to the business. It connects "what shareholders want" to "which APO/BAI/DSS objectives matter most" for your specific enterprise.
