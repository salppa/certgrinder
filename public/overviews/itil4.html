🏗️ The Service Value System (SVS)

The SVS is the top-level ITIL 4 model. It converts Opportunity & Demand (inputs) into Value (output) through five interconnected components. Everything else in ITIL sits inside the SVS.

🧭 Guiding Principles
7 universal recommendations that guide decisions in any situation. Cannot be overridden by management. Apply to all stakeholders.
🏛️ Governance
Evaluation, direction, and monitoring by the governing body. Ensures the organisation aligns with strategy and regulatory requirements.
🔄 Service Value Chain
6 interconnected activities that combine to create value. The operating model of the SVS. Activities combine in "value streams".
⚙️ Practices (34)
Sets of organisational resources (people, processes, tools, information) designed to perform work. Categorised in 3 groups.
📈 Continual Improvement
Recurring activity at every level. The 7-step Continual Improvement Model guides iterative improvement aligned with the organisation's vision.
Exam tip: Inputs to the SVS = Opportunity AND Demand. Output = Value. The SVS exists to convert one into the other.

🔄 The Service Value Chain (SVC)

Six activities that combine flexibly into value streams to address different scenarios (e.g., a "user support" value stream combines Engage → Deliver & Support). All six activities interact with Practices and with each other.

🧭 The 7 Guiding Principles

1Focus on Value
Everything maps to value for stakeholders. Always ask: how does this help create value for customers and other stakeholders?
2Start Where You Are
Don't discard what exists. Assess current state objectively. Preserve and reuse successful practices and services before redesigning.
3Progress Iteratively with Feedback
Work in small, manageable iterations. Use feedback at every step to course-correct. Resist the urge to do everything at once.
4Collaborate and Promote Visibility
Involve the right stakeholders. Make work visible. Hidden work and hidden decisions are risks. Break down silos.
5Think and Work Holistically
No component works in isolation. Consider end-to-end impacts across all four dimensions. Avoid local optimisations that harm the whole.
6Keep it Simple and Practical
Use the minimum steps needed. Eliminate processes that add no value. Simple solutions are easier to maintain and improve.
7Optimise and Automate
First optimise (simplify), then automate. Automating a broken process just makes it fail faster. Maximise human value-add work.

🔲 The 4 Dimensions

Every service and the SVS must be considered from all four perspectives. PESTLE forces (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental) act as external constraints on all four dimensions.

👥 Organisations & People
Culture, structure, skills, roles, responsibilities. Do people have the capabilities needed? Is the culture aligned with strategy?
💻 Information & Technology
Information managed by services, and technology required. AI, databases, communication platforms, ITSM tools, analytics.
🤝 Partners & Suppliers
Other organisations involved in service delivery — vendors, cloud providers, outsourcers. Managed via supplier and contract management.
🔁 Value Streams & Processes
How activities are coordinated and sequenced. Defines workflows, procedures, and how inputs become outputs.

⚙️ The 34 Practices — Key Ones to Know

Service Management (must-know for exam)

PracticeKey Point
Incident Management ⭐Restore normal service ASAP. Goal = restoration, NOT root cause. Prioritise by Impact × Urgency.
Problem Management ⭐Find and eliminate root causes. Incident = symptom. Problem = root cause. Known Error = problem with known workaround.
Change Enablement ⭐3 types: Standard (pre-approved, low risk), Normal (goes through CAB), Emergency (bypass for urgency). Goal: maximise successful changes.
Service Desk ⭐Single point of contact. Handles incidents AND service requests. Can be local, centralised, virtual, or follow-the-sun.
Service Level Management ⭐Set, monitor, and report SLAs. Balance business expectations with IT capabilities. Uses XLAs (experience level agreements) in modern ITIL.
Service Request ManagementPredefined, low-risk requests (password reset, new laptop). Separate from incident management.
Monitoring & Event ManagementObserve services, detect events, categorise (informational/warning/exception), trigger appropriate action.
Service Configuration ManagementCMDB — Configuration Management Database. Records CIs (Configuration Items) and their relationships. Feeds other practices.
IT Asset ManagementManages full lifecycle of IT assets (hardware, software licences). Financial accountability and compliance.
Availability ManagementEnsure services deliver agreed availability. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failures), MTRS (Mean Time to Restore Service).

General Management (key ones)

PracticeKey Point
Continual Improvement ⭐7-step model: Vision → Current state → Target → Plan → Take action → Check → Keep momentum. Applies everywhere.
Information Security ManagementProtect CIA: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. Also Authentication and Non-repudiation in modern versions.
Risk ManagementIdentify, assess, respond to risks. Supports all other practices. Risk = uncertainty affecting outcomes.
Relationship ManagementEstablish and nurture links between provider and stakeholders at strategic level (≠ Service Level Management which is operational).
Supplier ManagementManage supplier performance. Supplier contracts, SLAs with third parties. Ensures suppliers meet commitments.

💡 Core Concepts

Utility & Warranty
Utility = what it does (fit for purpose). Warranty = how it performs (fit for use: available, secure, sufficient capacity, continuous). Both are required for value.
Output vs Outcome
Output = tangible deliverable. Outcome = result for the stakeholder. ITIL focuses on outcomes. Customers buy outcomes, not outputs.
Value Co-creation
Value is not delivered TO customers — it is co-created WITH them. The consumer must actively participate in value creation (e.g. use the service correctly).
Service Relationship
Service Provider ↔ Service Consumer. Consumer roles: Customer (defines requirements), User (uses service), Sponsor (authorises budget). One person can play multiple roles.
